# Booking conversion — turning a visit into an appointment

## Booking CTA copy that converts
A booking call-to-action names the outcome, not the mechanism: "Book your first
class", "Reserve a table", "Get your quote" beat "Submit" or "Contact us" every
time. Use one primary booking CTA per page, repeated at natural decision points
(after the offer, after the proof, at the end) — the same words each time, so
the action feels like one door, not five. On the home page, the hero's CTA
should already be the booking action; never make a visitor hunt the contact
page to find out how to buy. Pair the CTA with a friction-remover in the same
breath: "Book a visit — takes 30 seconds, no payment now."

## Trust immediately before the ask
The sentences directly above a booking form do more selling than the form.
Answer, in one or two short lines, the visitor's last doubts: what happens
after they book ("We confirm within 2 hours on WhatsApp"), what it costs to
try ("First consultation is free"), and that a real person is on the other
end ("You'll hear from Priya, not a bot"). A specific, verifiable promise
("Replies within 2 business hours") converts better than a warm platitude
("We'd love to hear from you"). Never stack testimonials right on top of the
form — one strong, named line of proof is worth three anonymous ones.

## The response-time promise
Every enquiry page should state when the visitor will hear back, in their
timezone reality: "Message us before 6 pm and we'll reply the same day."
This single line measurably lifts form completion because it converts an
open-ended wait into a known one. Make the promise the business can keep —
an honest "within a day" beats a broken "within the hour". After the form is
sent, the confirmation should repeat the promise: "Booking received — we'll
get back to you by this evening."

## Offering choices inside a booking form
When a business has distinct services (haircut vs bridal styling, group class
vs personal training), letting the visitor pick the service in the form makes
the follow-up call shorter and signals professionalism. But choice must never
add friction: one dropdown with the owner's own service names, an easy
"Not sure yet" escape, and no forced choice when the visitor just wants to
talk. The service names should match the words used on the services page —
a visitor who read "Bridal styling" must not face "Package C" in the form.
