# Design craft for generated websites

## Ground every design in the subject's world
Distinctive choices come from the business's own materials, instruments and
vernacular — a tattoo studio draws from ink, iron and skin; a bakery from
crumb, flour and dawn light. Name the subject, its audience and the page's
single job before choosing anything visual. Never design for "a business";
design for THIS one.

## Avoid the AI-default looks
Generated design clusters around three templated looks: warm cream with a
serif display and terracotta accent; near-black with one acid-green or
vermilion pop; broadsheet hairlines with dense columns. These are defaults,
not choices. If the brand brief doesn't demand one, spend the freedom on a
palette derived from the subject's actual world instead.

## The hero is a thesis
Open with the most characteristic thing in the subject's world — a claim,
an image, a moment. A big number with a small label and a gradient accent
is the template answer. The headline must pass the "so what" test in three
seconds and belong to this business alone.

## Typography carries the personality
Pair a characterful display face with a complementary body face —
deliberately, not the same families every project reaches for. Set a clear
scale with intentional weights and spacing. The type treatment itself
should be memorable, not a neutral container.

## One signature, everything else quiet
Spend boldness in exactly one place — a signature element the page is
remembered by — and keep everything around it disciplined. Cut any
decoration that does not serve the brief. Before shipping, remove one
accessory.

## Palette as a system, not a color
A palette is 4-6 named values with jobs: background, surface, ink, accent.
Derive them from the subject (espresso browns for a coffee bar, sage and
sand for yoga) and keep contrast honest — ink on background must stay
readable, the accent must work at button size.

## Motion is seasoning
One orchestrated moment (a page-load settle, a scroll reveal) lands harder
than scattered effects. Hover states acknowledge the cursor; transitions
share one easing so the page feels like one object. Excess animation reads
as AI-generated. Always respect reduced-motion preferences.

## Copy is design material
Words exist to make the page easier to use. Active voice; a control says
exactly what happens ("Book a class", never "Submit"). Name things by what
customers recognize, not how the business is organized. Being specific
always beats being clever.
