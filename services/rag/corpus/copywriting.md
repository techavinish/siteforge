# Small-business website copywriting

## Headlines that convert
A homepage headline must pass the "so what" test in three seconds: name the
customer's desired outcome, not the business's category. "Fresh bread worth
crossing town for" beats "Welcome to Sharma Bakery". Avoid greeting words
(welcome, hello) — they waste the most valuable pixels on the page. Keep
headlines under 9 words; put the emotion in the headline and the proof in
the sub-line beneath it.

## Specificity beats adjectives
Generic praise ("high quality", "best in town", "excellent service") is
invisible to readers. Replace every adjective with a verifiable detail:
"48-layer croissants laminated every morning at 5am" outsells "delicious
fresh pastries". Numbers, place names, process details, and named people
create trust that adjectives cannot.

## Calls to action
Every page needs exactly one primary action, phrased as the thing the
customer gets, not the thing they do: "Get my free quote" beats "Submit".
Place a CTA above the fold and repeat it at the bottom. On contact pages,
reduce friction by stating response time: "We reply within 2 hours."

## Tone consistency
Pick the tone from the owner's own words and hold it on every page. Warm
and friendly means contractions, first person, and short sentences. Premium
and elegant means longer rhythm, no exclamation marks, restrained claims.
Never mix registers across pages — the About page and Menu must sound like
the same person.

## The About page formula
People buy from people. Structure: one line on why the business exists
(the founding moment), one on what makes the craft different, one on the
people, one inviting the reader in. Under 150 words. First person singular
for solo owners, first person plural for teams.
