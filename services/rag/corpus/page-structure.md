# Page structure patterns that work

## Homepage
Order: hero headline + one-line promise → three proof points (what makes
this business different, each one sentence) → most popular offerings with
prices → one testimonial-style line → CTA. Homepages fail by trying to say
everything; they succeed by making one promise and proving it three ways.

## Services or menu page
Group items in threes to sevens; more becomes a wall. Each item: name the
customer would say, one appetite-or-benefit line, a price. Show prices —
hiding them costs more enquiries than it protects. Lead each group with
its most profitable item, not alphabetical order.

## Contact page
The shortest page and the highest stakes. Address, phone as a tappable
line, hours, and what happens after they reach out ("we reply within 2
hours"). Add one warm sentence — a contact page with personality converts
measurably better than a bare form.

## Pricing psychology
Anchor with the premium option first. Odd, specific prices read as honest
(₹1,850 beats ₹2,000). Never write "prices may vary" without a starting
point — "from ₹500" keeps trust while allowing range.
